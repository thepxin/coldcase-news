The NVIDIA Board of Directors announced on November 8 that it is initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the inquiry ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). In a memo to employees, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029595/full-text-jensen-huang-affair-memo-nvidia)).

## Details (as Reported)

The board's statement says Huang fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). The board also stated that it prioritizes U.S. national and economic security and will provide further updates as appropriate ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)).

The New York Times reported that US intelligence officials believed Huang had been the target of a Chinese spy operation, centered on Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company that has bought billions of dollars of Nvidia's chips ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-summoned-by-congress-over-chinese-espionage-concerns/)). A memo circulating among U.S. policymakers earlier this year claimed that Alice Huang had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia's CEO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy/)).

Nvidia rejected the claims, stating that meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job and that Huang talks with thousands of customer executives every year ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-summoned-by-congress-over-chinese-espionage-concerns/)). Earlier, the company described the pair's interactions as not unusual and stated that it does not share classified information with customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy/)).

A spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington dismissed the allegations in late October as "groundless smears and slanders," and no one has been charged ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/is-jensen-huangs-job-on-the-line-yales-jeffrey-sonnenfeld-estimates-its-about-a-50-50/)). Nvidia stated on Sunday that Huang will keep running the company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/is-jensen-huangs-job-on-the-line-yales-jeffrey-sonnenfeld-estimates-its-about-a-50-50/)).
