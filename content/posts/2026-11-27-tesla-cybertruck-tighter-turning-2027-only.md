Tesla announced on its official Cybertruck account that an update tightening the truck's turning circle is rolling out, and added that it requires software version 2026.44 or newer and applies only to model year 2027 Cybertrucks ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)). In early 2024, a Tesla engineer stated that a software update would raise the rear wheel steering angle from about 3 degrees at launch to 10 degrees, and Elon Musk described improvements in the works to "tighten the turning circle significantly" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)). The company has not said whether the update delivers the full 10 degrees ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)).

## Timeline

- **2026-11-21**: Tesla's official Cybertruck account posted a drone video comparing the turning path before and after, with the caption "Even tighter turns now rolling out to new Cybertrucks" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)).
- **2026-11-25**: TechCrunch reported that used Cybertrucks averaged 39.1 days on the market in October 2026, compared with 65.5 days for trucks overall ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/the-canceled-tesla-cybertruck-is-the-fastest-selling-used-truck-in-america/)).

## Details (as Reported)

Electrek described the video as showing a truck circling in a parking lot, with an "after" circle that looks to be at least a foot smaller than the "before" circle ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)). The Cybertruck's maneuverability comes from rear wheel steering paired with steer-by-wire, and at low speeds the rear wheels turn opposite to the fronts ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)).

Before the update, Tesla's owner's manual listed the curb-to-curb turning circle at about 43.5 feet, against roughly 47.8 feet for the Ford F-150 Lightning and 44.9 feet for the Rivian R1T ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)). Electrek reported that Tesla plans to stop building the truck by the end of next summer, after Musk pushed the end date to work through an order backlog ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)). It also reported that Tesla now sells only the $109,990 Cyberbeast ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-cybertruck-tighter-turning-radius-update/)).

In the used market, iSeeCars.com found the Cybertruck was the fastest-selling used truck in October 2026, ahead of the Ford Maverick Hybrid and the Rivian R1T ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/the-canceled-tesla-cybertruck-is-the-fastest-selling-used-truck-in-america/)).
