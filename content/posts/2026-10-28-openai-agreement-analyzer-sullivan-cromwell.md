Law firm Sullivan & Cromwell developed Agreement Analyzer, an AI tool for identifying issues in a draft agreement, in collaboration with OpenAI, according to an OpenAI page dated 2026-10-15 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). On 2026-10-21, OpenAI described how AT&T is building on LegalEdge, its team applying AI to major legal matters, to support its in-house legal department ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/att-legal)).

Sullivan & Cromwell partner John Savva developed Agreement Analyzer as a single-user proof of concept built with Codex ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). He then worked with OpenAI on output quality, efficiency through parallel processing and a version that multiple lawyers could use at the same time ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)).

## Details (as Reported)

According to OpenAI, Agreement Analyzer integrates Sullivan & Cromwell's workflows, curated firm precedents, negotiating playbooks and ChatGPT Enterprise ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). After a lawyer inputs a draft agreement, the lawyer can add selected precedents, a playbook with firm and client positions, and information on market practice ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). The tool performs multiple review cycles from different perspectives and synthesizes the outputs ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)).

Savva described an application in which agents develop conclusions and other agents challenge them in an adversarial process ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). The results distinguish findings supported after challenge, dismissed findings, and unverified issues retained with qualifications ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)). He also stated that an AI tool's output is trustworthy only with full transparency, and he pointed to reasoning advances in GPT-6 Astra ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sullivan-cromwell)).

AT&T's in-house legal department has hundreds of lawyers and legal professionals around the world ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/att-legal)). Chief Compliance Officer Bill Ryan described a planned intake system with a front door of natural language intake that would classify and prioritize requests ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/att-legal)). The system would also connect requests with relevant precedent, research and past work, and give the responsible legal professional historical context and recommended first steps ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/att-legal)). General Counsel David McAtee stated that the decision making will always remain with the lawyers ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/att-legal)).
