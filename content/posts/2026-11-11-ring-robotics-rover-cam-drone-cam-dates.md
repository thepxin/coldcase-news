Amazon's Ring unveiled Ring Robotics on November 10, 2026, launching with the Ring Drone Cam, a flying indoor security camera, and the Ring Rover Cam, an autonomous security robot for business ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot)). Wired reports that the Rover Cam launches first on December 16, followed by the Drone Cam on January 27 ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/ring-robotics-drone-cam-rover-cam/)). The Rover Cam can be preordered now, while the Drone Cam has no preorder yet ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/10/rings-wheeled-and-flying-robots-will-patrol-your-home/)).

Amazon's release states that the device has not been authorized as required by the rules of the FCC and may not be offered for sale until authorization is obtained ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/10/ring-robots-ready-to-patrol-paranoids-pads/5308932)).

## Details (as Reported)

Both devices are powered by AI Pilot, which Amazon describes as a custom-built system using the devices' cameras, specialized vision models, on-device sensors and cloud processing ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot)). Setup turns a walkthrough of the space into a floor plan, and the system suggests patrol points ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot)).

The Drone Cam offers a 148-degree field of view, 360-degree rotation and 2K video ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot)). Wired reports it weighs 165 grams, flies five to eight minutes per charge, and lands on its own if it loses internet connectivity ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/ring-robotics-drone-cam-rover-cam/)). According to Wired, the rest of its intelligence sits in the cloud ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/ring-robotics-drone-cam-rover-cam/)). Ars Technica reports that set routes and schedules are available to subscribers of the Amazon Ring Protect plan ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/ring-cameras-go-mobile-as-amazon-debuts-ai-powered-security-drone-and-robot/)).

The Rover Cam streams and records in up to 4K video while moving or patrolling, and includes a periscope camera, siren, light tower and two-way audio ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot)). Wired reports that it covers up to 5,000 square feet and can patrol for about 30 minutes ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/ring-robotics-drone-cam-rover-cam/)).

Amazon told The Register that the Drone Cam would still start shipping in January ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/10/ring-robots-ready-to-patrol-paranoids-pads/5308932)). Ars Technica reports that the Rover Cam and Drone Cam are expected to reach customers at the end of 2026 and in early 2027, respectively ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/ring-cameras-go-mobile-as-amazon-debuts-ai-powered-security-drone-and-robot/)).

Ring says video is protected by Throw Away the Key Encryption, under which Ring temporarily holds keys to run cloud features before deleting its copy ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/10/ring-robots-ready-to-patrol-paranoids-pads/5308932)). Wired notes that this brief window makes it less foolproof than true end-to-end encryption ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/ring-robotics-drone-cam-rover-cam/)).
