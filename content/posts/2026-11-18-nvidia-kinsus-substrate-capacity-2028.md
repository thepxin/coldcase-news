Nvidia announced on 2026-11-17 expanded commitments with supply chain partners, including a new multiyear agreement under which Kinsus plans to expand its advanced ABF package substrate manufacturing capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5x over current levels ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). The expansion is planned at the Youth campus in Taoyuan, Taiwan ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). Production is expected to begin in the second half of 2027, and the additional capacity is targeted for the third quarter of 2028 ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)).

## Details (as Reported)

The Kinsus agreement builds on the companies' existing supplier relationship and supports Nvidia's long-term supply planning for its accelerated computing platforms ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). Kinsus CEO and president Scott Chen said that after more than a decade of partnership with Nvidia, the company is excited to continue building the future together ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)).

Nvidia and Samsung Electro-Mechanics amended their existing substrate supply agreement to improve operational capabilities at existing factories ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). The amendment builds on a previously announced collaboration covering substrate manufacturing in Sejong and Busan, South Korea ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)).

On packaging, Nvidia and SPIL are expanding advanced packaging operations in Taiwan under a multiyear agreement supported by multibillion-dollar funding from Nvidia ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). The project includes new production lines spanning bumping, interposer wafer fabrication, assembly and testing ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). Nvidia also supports Amkor's expansion in Arizona and South Korea through agreements announced in July and October 2026, totaling nearly $3 billion and including prepayments ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)).

The commitments follow October announcements with Amkor, IBIDEN, King Yuan Electronics, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL and Unimicron ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). Those plans included an aggregate 2.5x increase in substrate capacity over three years and a doubling of semiconductor testing capacity from 2026 to 2027 ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)). Nvidia states that Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs began volume shipments in the third quarter, using TSMC's 5nm, 3nm and CoWoS advanced packaging technologies ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout)).
