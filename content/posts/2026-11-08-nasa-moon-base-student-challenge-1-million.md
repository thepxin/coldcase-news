NASA announced on Sunday the opening of its $1 million Moon Base Student Challenge during a MAX POWER event at Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex in Florida ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). The competition invites student teams at U.S. high schools and post-secondary schools to contribute designs for the Moon Base ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). Participants must register through the NASA STEM Gateway, and registration is open until Jan. 25, 2027 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)).

The agency is building a Moon Base where astronauts will live, work, and explore at the lunar South Pole region ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). Student teams will design solutions across six categories: power generation and storage, construction, life-support systems, surface mobility, communications networks, and astronaut safety ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)).

## Details (as Reported)

NASA describes the effort as a multiphase challenge that supports its commitment to cultivating the technical skills of tomorrow's aerospace workforce ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). The deliverables reflect industry skills such as trade studies, concept proposals, and stakeholder briefings ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). According to the agency, this lets participants across technical, vocational, and various academic tracks build career-relevant skills without prior specialized engineering or technical experience ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)).

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said, "You do not have to wait until graduation to contribute" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). Carlos García-Galán, manager of the Moon Base Program, described students as bringing "exactly the bold ideas that we're looking for" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). Elaine Ho, associate administrator for the Office of STEM Engagement at NASA Headquarters, called students "the next generation of explorers and innovators" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)).

The Office of STEM Engagement designed the challenge in collaboration with the Moon Base Program ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)). The Institute of Competition Sciences administers it through a contract with the NASA Center of Excellence for Collaborative Innovation within the agency's Research and Technology Mission Directorate ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-1-million-to-students-for-moon-base-challenge/)).
