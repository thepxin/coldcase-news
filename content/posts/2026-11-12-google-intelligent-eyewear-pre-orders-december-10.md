Google opened pre-orders on 2026-11-12 for its new Intelligent Eyewear collections, made in collaboration with Gentle Monster, Warby Parker and Samsung, and announced that the glasses will be available to purchase in stores and online on December 10 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). Pre-orders are live online and in-store at Gentle Monster and Warby Parker ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). 9to5Google reports the same retail date of December 10 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/12/google-intelligent-eyewear-glasses/)).

## Details (as Reported)

Google said the Gentle Monster collection includes six designs and starts at $449, and will be available in the United States and in South Korea ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). The Warby Parker lineup has three styles, Alden, Reid and Wren, in optical or sun versions starting at $395 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). Customers can sign up for a guided demo at more than 360 Warby Parker retail locations ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)).

The company described frames weighing around 41 grams and a battery that lasts up to nine hours, with a charging case that can recharge the frames up to seven times ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). 9to5Google lists the frames at 40-44 grams and a 245mAh battery rated at nine hours ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/12/google-intelligent-eyewear-glasses/)).

The glasses carry a 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera and work with Pixel and Samsung phones as well as iPhones ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/)). 9to5Google reports the companion app supports Pixel 7 and newer, recent Galaxy models running One UI 9.0 or higher, and iPhone 13 Pro and up ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/12/google-intelligent-eyewear-glasses/)). It also lists Android XR running on the Qualcomm Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 with 2GB of RAM and 64GB of storage ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/12/google-intelligent-eyewear-glasses/)).

Engadget, after hands-on testing, reports that Google handles the operating system, Samsung handles much of the hardware, and Warby Parker and Gentle Monster handle design, lenses, sales and customer support ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2314258/android-xr-smartglasses-hands-on/)). It also reports that no paid Gemini plan is required to use the glasses' features ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2314258/android-xr-smartglasses-hands-on/)).

On privacy, Google stated that an outward-facing indicator light turns on when a photo or video is taken for the gallery, and that the glasses are designed to redact bystanders' faces before Gemini processes visuals ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/intelligent-eyewear-privacy-protections/)). Every pair also has a physical power switch ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/intelligent-eyewear-privacy-protections/)).
