Anthropic launched Personal Finance in Claude on November 5, 2026, letting users connect bank and card accounts and ask questions about their money in their own words ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)). The feature is rolling out in the US to paid subscribers ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)). Anthropic describes the access as read-only, and it states that Personal Finance cannot move, manage or invest money ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)).

## Details (as Reported)

Accounts are connected only after the user signs in to the bank through Plaid, a service that connects apps to banks ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)). Anthropic states that this is where the user agrees to Plaid's terms and approves Claude's read-only access, so Claude does not ask for or see the bank password ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)). Claude can also present the data in dashboards, calendars and more ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)).

Anthropic cautions that Personal Finance is not designed to give investment advice and that Claude can make mistakes ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)). It advises users to ask which transactions are behind a number and to check important figures with their bank ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/)).

Engadget describes the feature as a rival to OpenAI's Finances in ChatGPT, which debuted in May ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)). Both tools answer general questions about financial goals, generate dashboards, use Plaid and have read-only access, according to Engadget ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)).

Anthropic states that conversations in the Personal Finance hub are not used to train its models by default, and that Claude no longer sees previously provided data if the user unlinks the bank account ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)). OpenAI states that finance conversations follow the model training settings chosen across ChatGPT, where the option called Improve the model for everyone is on by default ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)). OpenAI also states that synced account data is deleted from its systems within 30 days of disconnecting ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)).

Anthropic states that its feature has no ads or sponsored recommendations and that the company does not offer credit cards, loans or savings accounts of its own ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2310182/claude-vs-chatgpt-personal-finance-comparison/)).
