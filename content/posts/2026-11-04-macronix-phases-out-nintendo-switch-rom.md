On November 4, 2026, Engadget reported that Macronix, the Taiwanese semiconductor and memory manufacturer it describes as Nintendo's main Switch cartridge supplier, is phasing out its ROM production, a move confirmed by CEO Miin Wu ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)). The Verge reported that the company announced the gradual phase-out during a recent quarterly earnings call and is instead focusing on NOR flash memory for data centers ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)).

## Details (as Reported)

Engadget reports that the ROM business has declined by 39 percent over the past year and now accounts for five percent of Macronix sales, down from 26 percent at this time last year ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)). A post embedded in the report states that there will be no new ROM products and that Q3 is the last quarter in which Macronix reports ROM sales separately ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)).

According to Taipei Times as cited by The Verge, Macronix chairman and CEO Miin Wu said during its Q3 2026 earnings call: "Customers are chasing us for more capacity, demanding that we deliver more NOR flash solutions for data centers" ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)). The company also reportedly expects chip price hikes to persist ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)).

Engadget notes that Macronix is far from the only entity helping to create physical Switch cartridges ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)). It also reports that Switch 2 cartridges use NAND flash memory, a segment Macronix is not phasing out, so the change does not affect them in quite the same way ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)). The embedded post describes Macronix as making the ROM chips in Switch 1 and Switch 2 game memory cards ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)).

On physical media, The Verge reports that Nintendo has recently said it has "no plans" to ditch physical games ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)). Nintendo stated in August that physical titles accounted for 38.5 percent of its games sales ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)). Engadget puts digital products at 61.5 percent of Nintendo's software sales and around 85 percent on PS5 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2306886/nintendos-main-switch-cartridge-supplier-is-leaving-the-rom-business/)). Sony has announced a plan to stop production of physical game discs starting in January 2028 ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)).

The Verge adds that the exit comes nearly a year after Micron shut down Crucial, its consumer RAM and SSD brand, to meet AI data center demand ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1026426/nintendo-switch-cartridges-rom-supplier-data-center)).
