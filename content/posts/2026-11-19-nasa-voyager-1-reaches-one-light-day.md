## Details (as Reported)

NASA defined a light-day as the distance light travels in 24 hours, or 16,094,799,096 miles ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/)). The agency stated that Voyager 1 would have taken 49 years, two months and 11 days to travel that distance ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/)). Tom's Hardware put the spacecraft's speed at 37,844 mph and reported that NASA has confirmed Voyager 1 as the first human-made object to reach one light-day ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/space/voyager-1-spacecraft-crosses-milestones-distance-of-one-light-day-from-earth-49-years-after-launch-now-16-09-billion-miles-away-signal-takes-full-24-hours-to-travel-home)).

Tom's Hardware reports a confirmation, while Engadget reports that the exact time cannot be confirmed until the probe's message reaches Earth ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2320267/nasa-voyager-1-space-probe-one-light-day-away-from-earth/)).

Voyager mission manager Kareem Badaruddin told Wired that the day of lag now affects operations and that building new commands has become a week-long process ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/voyager-1-beat-still-working-light-day-away/)). In September, mission operations switched off some devices in favor of lower-power alternatives, a maneuver the team called the "Big Bang" that saved 10 watts ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/voyager-1-beat-still-working-light-day-away/)).

NASA stated that Voyager 1 is headed toward the star Gliese 445 and is expected to pass within about 1.7 light-years of it in 40,000 years ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/)). Voyager 2 is on pace to be one light-day away in November 2035, according to the agency ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- The exact time Voyager 1 crossed one light-day, which NASA cannot confirm until the probe's message arrives the next day, according to Engadget ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2320267/nasa-voyager-1-space-probe-one-light-day-away-from-earth/)).
- How long the mission continues: Badaruddin's best guess is that it could end in the next three to five years, and he acknowledged that earlier predictions of a few remaining years were proven wrong ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/voyager-1-beat-still-working-light-day-away/)).
