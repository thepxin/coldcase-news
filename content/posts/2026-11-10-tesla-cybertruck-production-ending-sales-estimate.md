Tesla CEO Elon Musk announced on Monday that the company will shut down Cybertruck production in about eight months, years after he predicted annual sales of around 250,000 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/musk-says-tesla-will-refund-some-cybertruck-deposits-defends-decision-to-end-production/)). Industry experts estimate about 71,000 Cybertrucks have been sold since the first deliveries in November 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/musk-says-tesla-will-refund-some-cybertruck-deposits-defends-decision-to-end-production/)). The first announcement came on Friday, when Musk said Tesla would wind down production at Giga Texas around mid-2027 "to make room for increased Cybercab production" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)).

## Details (as Reported)

In his post, Musk wrote that the order rate "far exceeds our production rate" and that the move is "not a case of Cybertruck being unprofitable" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)). He argued that the opportunity cost of the truck's complexity is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/musk-says-tesla-will-refund-some-cybertruck-deposits-defends-decision-to-end-production/)).

Electrek disputed the demand claim, reporting that the Cybertruck line has been running at roughly a quarter of its installed capacity ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)). Tesla's latest shareholder letter still lists more than 125,000 units of installed capacity, according to Electrek ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)). Tesla delivered 8,295 "Other Models" in Q3 2026, and Electrek estimated 7,000 to 8,000 of those were Cybertrucks ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)).

Cox Automotive estimates US Cybertruck sales fell from 14,416 in Q3 2024 to 3,519 in Q1 2026 and 3,744 in Q2 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)). Electrek also reported that Tesla raised Cybertruck prices by up to 10% and paused orders for the cheapest trim on the day production was announced to end ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)). The cheapest orderable Cybertruck went from $69,990 in early August to $92,490, the outlet reported ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)).

SpaceX purchased $131 million worth of Cybertrucks from Tesla in 2025, according to its S-1 registration statement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/musk-says-tesla-will-refund-some-cybertruck-deposits-defends-decision-to-end-production/)). Wired reported that the truck's divisive design, Elon Musk's embrace of you name it and a number of recalls contributed to subpar sales ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/cybertrucks-death-great-news-for-evs/)). Wired noted the Cybertruck uses a 123-kilowatt-hour battery, while the Cybercab battery is 48 kilowatt-hours ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/cybertrucks-death-great-news-for-evs/)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- Musk's claim that orders far exceed production is disputed by Electrek, which cites Tesla's delivery figures and capacity ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)).
- Tesla does not disclose Cybertruck margins, so Musk's statement that the truck is not unprofitable cannot be checked, according to Electrek ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/09/musk-cybertruck-demand-claim-data/)).
- TechCrunch reported that sales could see a final bump as the truck goes out of production, though even a scarcity boost is unlikely to reach the projections made several years ago ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/musk-says-tesla-will-refund-some-cybertruck-deposits-defends-decision-to-end-production/)).
