YouTube is preparing to pay around $3 billion to thousands of US school districts to settle claims that its video platform harms the mental health of students, Bloomberg reported ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)). Sources told Bloomberg that YouTube reached the agreement "in principle" ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)). The deal would also require YouTube to introduce new safeguards for teens ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)). Google did not immediately respond to The Verge's request for comment ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).

## Details (as Reported)

The districts are seeking reimbursement for the counseling services they have had to supply to students, according to Bloomberg ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)). A $3 billion settlement would be the largest ever reached with US school districts, Bloomberg reported ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)). The agreement would allow Google to avoid a wave of lawsuits filed by US school districts against YouTube, Meta, Snap and TikTok ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).

The Verge reported that the first bellwether trial was due to start in June and that the companies settled for a total of $27 million ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)). Engadget reported that the settlement came in May and that YouTube agreed to pay slightly more than $2 million ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)).

YouTube was also found negligent in a personal-injury lawsuit in Los Angeles in March and agreed to settle a separate personal-injury lawsuit in June ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)).

In August, Meta agreed to pay $17.1 billion over 10 years to settle lawsuits filed by a group of 47 states, along with several districts and territories, according to The Verge ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)). Engadget described the Meta figure as up to $18 billion paid to states ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)). Meta will pay the remaining $5.3 billion only if YouTube and TikTok roll out similar changes and pay matching amounts ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- Google had not responded to The Verge's request for comment ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).
- Engadget asked YouTube to comment on the school district settlement ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)).
- Bloomberg reported that the settlement needs a few months to officially finalize and depends on a majority of districts agreeing ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2323927/youtube-could-pay-3-billion-to-settle-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-with-school-districts/)).
- The next trial, set for February 2027 before an Oakland, California, judge, names Meta, Snap and TikTok as defendants ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).
