SpaceX caught the Starship upper stage with the arms of its launch tower in South Texas on Flight 15, the first time the Ship has been caught, while the Super Heavy V3 booster aborted its own catch and exploded over the Gulf ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316926/watch-spacex-attempt-to-catch-the-starship-upper-stage-for-the-first-time/)). Ars Technica had previewed the attempt as an effort to recover an orbital rocket fully with the intent of reusing all of its components ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/rocket-report-starship-set-for-historic-test-flight-a-century-of-electron/)).

## Details (as Reported)

Starship lifted off on Friday from South Texas on its 15th test flight, and SpaceX had planned its first double catch, in which the Super Heavy V3 booster would return to be caught by the chopstick arms before the Ship attempted the same maneuver a day later ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316926/watch-spacex-attempt-to-catch-the-starship-upper-stage-for-the-first-time/)). The first stage released the upper stage above the Gulf of Mexico, and the upper stage powered into a nominal orbit, the second time SpaceX has flown Starship into low-Earth orbit ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/)).

The deployment of 26 Starlink V3 satellites came about 40 minutes into the flight, and the satellites carry solar panels that stretch 60 meters end to end when fully deployed ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/)). Ars Technica reports that the first stage dropped quickly on its return but, due to an unspecified issue, was commanded to divert into the Gulf, where it caused a powerful explosion visible from more than 10 miles away ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/)). Engadget reports that the booster exploded before hitting the water ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316926/watch-spacex-attempt-to-catch-the-starship-upper-stage-for-the-first-time/)).

The Ship stayed in orbit for roughly 22 hours after launch before heading back to the surface ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316926/watch-spacex-attempt-to-catch-the-starship-upper-stage-for-the-first-time/)). Ars Technica reports that three Raptor engines ignited as the Ship swung vertical, and that two nubs protruding about one foot on either side were its only purchase on the arms ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/with-starship-catch-spacex-still-shows-it-can-dazzle-the-world/)). The Verge describes the result as a milestone but not a complete success, because the company did not recover the entire launch vehicle ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/1033930/spacex-chopstick-starship-catch)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- SpaceX is still trying to determine what caused the booster to abort its landing and detonate over the Gulf of Mexico ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/1033930/spacex-chopstick-starship-catch)).
- SpaceX has landed and reflown earlier Starship boosters but has yet to do so with version 3 ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/)).
