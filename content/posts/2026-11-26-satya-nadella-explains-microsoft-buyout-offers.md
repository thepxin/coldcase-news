Microsoft chief executive officer Satya Nadella explained on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, why the company offered buyouts to 60,000 of its employees in October ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)).

Microsoft confirmed to TechCrunch on Monday, October 26, 2026, that it was offering buyouts to around 60,000 employees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)). In a post on the company's blog that day, Amy Coleman, executive vice president and chief people officer, wrote that eligible employees would receive an email with a link to their Voluntary Exit Offer details within the next few hours ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/)). She described the offer as new for the company and as "a new choice" for eligible employees who may be considering a future outside the company ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/)). The post added that Microsoft would keep helping people "focus on the work that matters most" and "build new skills" ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/)).

TechCrunch reported that the terms will be similar to the company's standard severance, and that its headcount as of the end of June was 223,000 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)).

Speaking at the DealBook Summit, the chief executive told Andrew Ross Sorkin that the buyouts were meant "to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He pointed to the team that builds Cowork, the artificial intelligence agent in Microsoft 365 apps, which consists of 11 developers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He described the team's work in these words: "They build a software factory that builds the product. They don't build the product" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He added: "That level of direction is what, by the way, is going to happen in all jobs" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)).

- On The Pragmatic Engineer podcast with Gergely Orosz the previous week, Nadella said, "We have created enormous anxiety in people sort of feeling that they don't have control," and tied it to "the way we have talked about this AI revolution" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)).
