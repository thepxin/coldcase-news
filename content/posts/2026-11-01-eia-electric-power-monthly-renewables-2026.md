The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its Electric Power Monthly with data for August 2026 on October 23, 2026 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/)). Electrek reported on October 27, 2026 that renewably generated electricity during the first two-thirds of 2026 was 10.2% greater than a year earlier, based on the EIA report and a review by the SUN DAY Campaign ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).

## Details (as Reported)

Electrek reported that growth was led by utility-scale solar, up 20.0%, followed by small-scale solar at 12.0%, wind at 7.8%, hydropower at 4.2% and geothermal at 3.8% ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)). From January to August 2026, wind produced 333,367 GWh, or 10.6% of total US generation, while utility-scale and small-scale solar combined produced 329,119 GWh, or 10.5% ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).

Output from natural gas and nuclear plants grew by 2.6% and 1.3% respectively, while coal output fell by 8.7% ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).

On capacity, Electrek reported that utility-scale solar grew by 27,141.7 MW between September 1, 2025, and August 31, 2026, while small-scale solar grew by 6,526.1 MW and wind by 9,979.1 MW, including 800 MW of offshore wind ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)). Coal capacity fell by 2,703.4 MW, nuclear capacity was unchanged and natural gas capacity rose by 5,316.0 MW over the same period ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).

Electrek reported that the EIA projects utility-scale renewables to add 53,117.3 MW over the next twelve months, with utility-scale solar adding 44,603.1 MW and wind adding 8,312.3 MW, including 3,355.0 MW of offshore wind ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)). The EIA projects no new nuclear capacity and a net decline of 1,997.0 MW in fossil fuel capacity ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)). It also foresees battery storage adding 23,656.2 MW by September 1, 2027, bringing the total to 77,753.2 MW ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- The EIA does not provide a forecast for small-scale solar capacity growth over the next twelve months, and the SUN DAY Campaign assumes an additional 6,000 MW or more ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).
- Electrek reported that, if small-scale solar grows by about 6,000 MW by September 1, 2027, renewable installed capacity would reach about 550,000 MW against a projected 518,312.8 MW for natural gas ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/27/renewables-to-reach-40-of-total-generating-capacity-beat-gas-within-a-year-eia/)).
- The EIA lists its next Electric Power Monthly release as November 24, 2026 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/)).
