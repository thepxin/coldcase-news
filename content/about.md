**ColdCaseTech** is an independent cybersecurity and cloud infrastructure intelligence desk. While mainstream tech media abandons major security incidents after 48 hours, we track the long-tail aftermath—forensic post-mortems, CVE remediation failures, SEC breach filings, and regulatory fallout—months later.

### Two kinds of cases
- **Tech cases (daily).** What a company or official body promised, announced or claimed (**then**), and what verified sources say actually happened (**now**): shipped, measured, delayed, changed or cancelled. Verdicts: `Delivered`, `Partially Delivered`, `Not Delivered`.
- **Security Friday (weekly).** What was first reported about a security incident (**then**), and what approved sources establish today (**now**). These pass the same automated checks and are then reviewed by an editor before publishing. Verdicts: `Verified`, `Partially True`, `Escalated`.

### How we verify
ColdCaseTech does not draw conclusions. Each case reports then, now, and one verdict, using only approved sources.

1. **Approved sources only.** Every case is built only from the sources listed below. Nothing else is used or linked.
2. **Corroboration.** A case needs at least two different outlets, including at least one Tier A source: the company's or organisation's own statement, or an official body.
3. **Then vs. now.** The sources must cover the incident at two different times, so the change in status is documented, not assumed.
4. **Claims are not facts.** An attacker's claim is not reported unless the organisation or an official body has confirmed it.
5. **Checked before publishing.** Every case is written with AI assistance from those sources. Before publishing it must pass an independent claim-by-claim check against the sources, and automatic checks that every link, figure, date and CVE ID appears in a source. If anything fails, the case is not published.
6. **Corrections.** If we get something wrong, we fix it and note the correction on the case.

**Tech verdicts:** `Delivered` (it arrived or performed as promised), `Partially Delivered` (late, less than promised, in fewer places, or measured below the claim), `Not Delivered` (cancelled, missed the promised date, or does not do what was promised). A company's own words count as its promise, never as proof it delivered.

**Security verdicts:** `Verified` (the early reports held up), `Partially True` (some held up, others were corrected, disputed or remain unconfirmed), `Escalated` (later sources show a larger scope or impact than first reported).
