# Microsoft 365 outage and FAA ground stop disrupt November operations

> **Then vs. Now (News report):**
> * **Then:** Routine network capacity upgrades, software updates, and automated monitoring operated across Microsoft 365 and U.S. aviation systems<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.
> * **Now:** Between November 12 and November 13, 2026, software defects and router instability disrupted Microsoft 365 globally and triggered a nationwide FAA ground stop, while officials reported no evidence of a cyberattack on either incident<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-2">2</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.

Microsoft reported a global Microsoft 365 disruption starting November 12, 2026, and the Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop on November 13, 2026<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>. Federal officials and reporting on the incidents attributed the disruptions to internal software defects and equipment issues rather than malicious cyberattacks<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-2">2</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.

## Timeline

* **2026-11-12**: Microsoft acknowledged Exchange Online mailbox access problems in the Americas at 17:54 UTC and warned of broader global issues at 18:07 UTC<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>.
* **2026-11-13**: Automated pool chlorine dosing malfunctioned at Hotel Ling Bao in Brühl, Germany, after an Outlook-based monitoring system could not send an alert email, leaving 15 people checked by paramedics and 5 injured<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>.
* **2026-11-13**: The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop around 3:30 p.m. ET due to a technical problem and resumed operations at 8:15 p.m. ET<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.
* **2026-11-14**: Microsoft 365 services were restored after a disruption spanning November 12 to November 14<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>.
* **2026-11-16**: Online banking access issues affected major banks, while public commentators questioned whether separate disruptions across banks, aviation, and software were linked<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a>, <a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>.

## Details (as Reported)

* Microsoft 365 serves about 430 million people globally, and search tools relying on Microsoft services, including Microsoft Bing, DuckDuckGo, and ChatGPT, also experienced problems during the outage<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>.
* At peak during the Microsoft outage, more than 80% of servers hosting the networking gateway service became unresponsive as a secure connection process became overwhelmed<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>.
* The Anderson Economic Group estimated lost productivity from the Microsoft outage at about $7 billion, while government agencies in Australia, Japan, the Philippines, Singapore, South Africa, and the United States reported disruptions<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>.
* Transportation officials stated that a contractor was replacing copper lines with fiber when a router began flooding others with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, while the FAA cited legacy TDM system instabilities<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.
* The FAA stated that some radio frequencies and radar feeds remained operational, critical life-saving, law enforcement, and military flights continued, and approximately 3,500 aircraft landed safely<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.
* Reporting noted that while overlapping alerts led some observers to suspect a coordinated attack, available information pointed to routine maintenance, separate incidents, or isolated issues rather than a single systemic collapse<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>.

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

* The FAA and L3Harris continue to investigate the root cause of the November 13 telecommunications instability<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a>, <a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>.
* Full final cost tallies across all affected enterprises and public agencies have not been established<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>, <a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>.
