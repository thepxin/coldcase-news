# Huawei Technologies convicted of Iran sanctions violations, fraud and trade secret conspiracy
type: report
then: The United States Department of Justice stated on November 24, 2026, that the defendants engaged in a decades-long scheme to enrich Huawei and grow its telecommunications business, including in the United States [United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy).
now: A federal jury in the Eastern District of New York convicted Huawei Technologies and three subsidiaries on November 24, 2026, and sentencing has been set for September 14, 2027 [United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy).
summary: Huawei Technologies and three subsidiaries were convicted of Iran sanctions violations, fraud, trade secret and money laundering conspiracies.

A federal jury in the Eastern District of New York convicted Huawei Technologies and three of its subsidiaries on November 24, 2026, following a three-month trial, of violating United States sanctions involving Iran, wire and bank fraud, conspiracy to steal trade secrets and money laundering conspiracy, according to the [United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy). The verdicts, returned by a federal jury in Brooklyn, cover the parent company and its subsidiaries Huawei Device, Futurewei Technologies and Huawei Device USA, as confirmed by the [United States Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/chinese-telecommunications-conglomerate-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions).

## Details (as Reported)

- The Justice Department reported that the company assisted the government of the Islamic Republic of Iran, and that it falsely told banks, journalists and the United States government that it did not assist Iran's government ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- According to the department, the company signed a non-disclosure agreement with T-Mobile while fully intending to violate the agreement, and sent photographs and technical details of Tappy, a robot used by T-Mobile to assess the responsiveness of touch screens, back to China ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- The department also reported that a Huawei employee stole a part of the robot in his backpack and carried it out of T-Mobile's controlled laboratory ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- The department reported that the company copied the source code used on Cisco routers, identified as the crown jewel of Cisco technology, left Cisco's notes about the Cisco code, and referred to Cisco using its name backwards, ocsic ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- According to the department, a Huawei employee caught photographing proprietary Fujitsu equipment had a badge falsely identifying him as an employee of Weihua rather than Huawei ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- The department stated that the company hired a researcher from a university to arrange collaboration with CNEX without disclosing that he was doing so on behalf of Huawei, and that the researcher then shared confidential information with Huawei and gave it direct access to CNEX's proprietary hardware ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- The department reported that the company obtained confidential information from Quintel, a United States antenna manufacturer, pursuant to a non-disclosure agreement, and filed a patent application attaching a confidential slide that Quintel provided to Huawei ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
- According to the department, the Federal Bureau of Investigation's New York Field Office is conducting the investigation, and attorneys from the Criminal Division, the National Security Division and the United States Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York are prosecuting the case ([United States Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy)).
