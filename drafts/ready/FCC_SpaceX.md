# SpaceX wins conditional approval for up to 1 million orbital data center satellites
type: report
then: SpaceX filed an application on January 30, 2026, seeking authority to launch and operate up to one million satellites as the SpaceX Orbital Data Center system [Federal Communications Commission](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-113A1.txt).
now: The Federal Communications Commission has given SpaceX conditional approval for a constellation of up to 1 million satellites, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026 [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/).
summary: SpaceX has conditional approval from the Federal Communications Commission for Starmind, a constellation of up to 1 million data center satellites.

The Federal Communications Commission has given SpaceX conditional approval for a constellation of up to 1 million satellites designed to serve as data centers in orbit, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026 [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/). Under the authorization, the company has seven years to place its first operational satellite in orbit and 14 years to deploy the entire constellation [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/).

SpaceX filed its application on January 30, 2026, and the Commission subsequently accepted it for filing and invited public comment [Federal Communications Commission](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-113A1.txt). The approval was granted despite objections from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, according to TechCrunch [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/).

## Details (as Reported)

- The Federal Communications Commission dismissed some of the key concerns raised by opponents, including potential threats to scientific research, because the agency determined they fell outside its regulatory authority, TechCrunch reported ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
- The objectors cited orbital debris and congestion, radiofrequency interference and environmental consequences, according to the same report ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
- The satellites, known as Starmind, would primarily communicate using lasers to connect with other satellites, including SpaceX's Starlink, according to TechCrunch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
- The approval allows SpaceX to use radiofrequency communications to maintain contact with and control its orbital data centers from Earth if their primary communications systems fail, TechCrunch reported ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
- The Commission's notice on the application stated that the proposed satellites will use high-bandwidth optical inter-satellite links ([Federal Communications Commission](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-113A1.txt)).

## What Is Not Yet Confirmed

- The Federal Communications Commission has not yet approved SpaceX's plans to handle orbital debris for satellites that would operate above 600 kilometers, TechCrunch reported ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
- SpaceX has requested a waiver of the rule that requires satellites ending their missions below 2,000 kilometers to reenter Earth's atmosphere within five years, according to TechCrunch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)).
