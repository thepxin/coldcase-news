# National Aeronautics and Space Administration targets 2030 nuclear reactor for the Moon

summary: The National Aeronautics and Space Administration seeks partners to develop and test Lunar Reactor-1, a fission power system for the Moon.

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration announced on November 23, 2026, that it is seeking industry partners to develop and test Lunar Reactor-1, a small fission power system for the Moon that it aims to have launch-ready by 2030 ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)). The space agency plans for the reactor to generate 20 kilowatts of electricity and to operate on the Moon for at least five years after deployment ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)). In January, the space agency and the United States Department of Energy made a renewed commitment to their longstanding partnership, with a memorandum of understanding that includes the development of a lunar surface reactor by 2030 ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-department-of-energy-to-develop-lunar-surface-reactor-by-2030/)).

## Details (as Reported)

- The solicitation incorporates feedback received after the space agency released a draft proposal in August ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)).
- Any proposed reactor must meet the energy department's safety standards for nuclear operations, according to the space agency ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)).
- The space agency describes Lunar Reactor-1 as a fission surface power system to keep its Moon Base operating through periods of darkness and in locations where solar power alone is not sufficient ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/space-reactor-1-freedom/)).
- Lunar Reactor-1 is managed by the space agency's Space Reactors Division in partnership with the energy department ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)).
- Space Reactor-1 Freedom, which the space agency is targeting for launch in late 2028, is built around a closed Brayton cycle power conversion system of 20 kilowatts electric, and its mission page describes it as demonstrating nuclear electric propulsion ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/space-reactor-1-freedom/)).
- The mission page states that Space Reactor-1 Freedom will inform and enable Lunar Reactor-1 ([National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/space-reactor-1-freedom/)).
