# Anthropic raises $81.3 billion, with $1.05 trillion to $1.08 trillion in infrastructure commitments
type: closed

Anthropic became a public company on Thursday, raising a total of $81.3 billion, including $1.95 billion for its seven founders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)). The company announced that its shares were expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026, under the symbol "ANT" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering)).

TechCrunch reported that the offering was priced at $83 a share, above the initial range of $70 to $78, and that 980 million shares were sold, up from the 825 million initially planned ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/?type=AI)). The sale surpassed the $75 billion record set by SpaceX to become the largest initial public offering in history by proceeds raised ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/?type=AI)).

On November 10, 2026, TechCrunch reported that each of the seven co-founders would sell 3,378,378 shares, and that at the midrange of $74 each founder would take in just shy of $250 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/all-7-anthropic-founders-will-each-cash-out-about-250m-in-ipo/)). At the final price of $83 a share, each founder took in about $280 million before underwriting fees and taxes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)).

According to TechCrunch, the company raised nearly $105 billion for its own coffers, which, together with capital it already had on hand, gives it nearly $230 billion of working capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)). Alongside the offering, the company sold a previously announced $26 billion worth of non-voting Class N stock to large technology companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)). The company has also disclosed infrastructure commitments of roughly $1.05 trillion to $1.08 trillion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)).

On November 20, 2026, Anthropic announced the closing of the offering, including the full exercise by the underwriters of their option to purchase an additional 147,000,000 shares of Class A common stock ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close)).

Amazon holds a 14.6 per cent stake and Google holds 13.4 per cent, and the two cloud providers together own 28 per cent of the company, TechCrunch reported ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/)).

Speaking ahead of the Nasdaq opening bell, the chief executive officer, Dario Amodei, pledged that "we will pace the training and deployment of AI systems" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/at-nasdaq-opening-bell-anthropic-ceo-recommits-to-pacing-ai-development/)). He added that the company would not bend to either business or political pressure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/at-nasdaq-opening-bell-anthropic-ceo-recommits-to-pacing-ai-development/)).
