# Meta launches Muse in the United Kingdom under a voluntary monitoring scheme
type: report

Meta announced on November 23, 2026, that Muse, its personal artificial intelligence agent, is available in the United Kingdom to anyone aged 18 and over on iOS, Android, muse.ai and WhatsApp ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/)).

In its launch announcement, the company described Muse as built "to be private, safe, and secure by design" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/)). Every Muse gets its own virtual computer in the cloud, and conversations with the agent, together with the data in its virtual machine, are not shared with Meta's advertising systems ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/)). The service is free to use, with optional subscription plans for those who want more usage ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/)).

A second announcement from Meta stated that a separate security agent checks Muse's actions and the information it sends out against the permissions a user has granted ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/muse-now-available-in-the-uk-muse-does-the-work-you-stay-in-control/)). The same announcement added: "We've engaged constructively with the appropriate regulatory authorities and will continue to do so" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/muse-now-available-in-the-uk-muse-does-the-work-you-stay-in-control/)).

On November 24, 2026, the Information Commissioner's Office published a statement on the launch under the name of its chief executive officer, Paul Arnold ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/statement-on-uk-launch-of-meta-muse/)). The regulator confirmed that it had been clear with Meta about its responsibilities under data protection law prior to launch, and that Meta has committed to a voluntary monitoring and reporting scheme with the regulator ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/statement-on-uk-launch-of-meta-muse/)). The statement also read: "We are monitoring the rollout of agentic AI products more broadly" ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/statement-on-uk-launch-of-meta-muse/)).

On the same day, the regulator reminded developers creating agentic artificial intelligence services for consumers to meet their data protection responsibilities, and set out steps for consumers, recommended together with the Competition and Markets Authority, which noted that "agents can make mistakes" ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/developers-creating-agentic-ai-services-for-consumers-reminded-to-meet-their-data-protection-responsibilities/)). The guidance warned that "Mistakes can be magnified if the agent is acting autonomously," and advised that users can start with the most limited mode ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/developers-creating-agentic-ai-services-for-consumers-reminded-to-meet-their-data-protection-responsibilities/)).

## Details (as Reported)

- TechCrunch reported that a separate Sentinel agent runs on the same virtual machine as Muse but is kept apart from Muse at the system level ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/meta-debuts-its-muse-ai-agent-will-consumers-trust-it/)).
- Meta stated that Muse cannot see a user's passwords or card details, and that it can check out with Onelink built by Stripe ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/)).
- On October 8, 2026, Richard Nevinson, Director of Technology Regulation at the Information Commissioner's Office, stated that "as AI systems operate with greater autonomy, robust data protection safeguards become even more critical" ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/10/ico-secures-changes-from-leading-ai-developers-as-scrutiny-extends-to-ai-agents/)).
- The regulator also confirmed enquiries with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and the AI Security Institute about recent agentic artificial intelligence testing and deployment ([Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/10/ico-secures-changes-from-leading-ai-developers-as-scrutiny-extends-to-ai-agents/)).
