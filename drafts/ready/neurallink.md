# Neuralink receives Food and Drug Administration approval to launch LINK pivotal study
type: report
then: Neuralink reported on February 5, 2025, that the first person received a Neuralink implant in January 2024 [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/a-year-of-telepathy/).
now: Neuralink announced on November 25, 2026, that it had received approval from the Food and Drug Administration to launch the LINK Pivotal Study for people with severe paralysis caused by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-to-launch-link-pivotal-study/).
summary: Neuralink announced Food and Drug Administration approval to launch the LINK Pivotal Study of its brain-computer interface.

Neuralink announced on November 25, 2026, that it had received approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration to launch the LINK Pivotal Study, a trial of its brain-computer interface system in people with severe paralysis caused by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-to-launch-link-pivotal-study/). The company described LINK as the first pivotal trial for an implantable brain-computer interface in the United States [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-to-launch-link-pivotal-study/).

Engadget reported on November 26, 2026, that the authorization was granted on an experimental basis [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/). According to the publication, the company can now recruit participants and conduct the study under an approved clinical protocol [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/).

In an update published on February 5, 2025, the company reported that the first person received a Neuralink implant in January 2024 [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/a-year-of-telepathy/). In its post on X, as reported by Engadget, the company stated that the new study builds on its work with more than 30 participants to date [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/).

## Details (as Reported)

- The company announced on X that LINK is "the first pivotal trial for an implantable brain-computer interface in the US" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/)).
- Engadget reported that patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis may be eligible for the LINK trial ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/)).
- Engadget reported that the study will assess the ability of patients to control digital devices through thought, as well as the system's long-term safety and reliability ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2329008/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-for-brain-implant-trial-in-als-patients/)).
- The company stated that its early feasibility studies in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom have included over 30 implanted participants ([Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-to-launch-link-pivotal-study/)).
- The company listed those affected by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis among the groups covered by its Breakthrough Device Designation for speech ([Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-breakthrough-device-designation-for-speech/)).
