# Department of Justice charges ransomware recovery co-founder with defrauding victims
type: report
then: According to the Department of Justice, a Florida data recovery company represented to ransomware victims that it never negotiated with or paid ransomware attackers ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
now: The Department of Justice has charged the company's co-founder with three counts of wire fraud, alleging that he secretly paid ransoms and charged his customers more ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/colombian-national-indicted-scheme-defraud-victims-ransomware-attacks)). 
summary: The Department of Justice charged a Florida data recovery company co-founder with wire fraud over alleged secret payments to ransomware attackers.

A federal grand jury in the District of Rhode Island returned an indictment on November 18, 2026, charging the co-founder of a Florida data recovery company, a 52-year-old Colombian national, with defrauding individuals and companies who were victims of ransomware attacks, the Department of Justice announced ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). According to the indictment, he negotiated with and paid ransomware attackers without the knowledge of his customers, then charged them a higher amount and kept the difference ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 

TechCrunch reported that the defendant is a former executive of a ransomware recovery firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/doj-accuses-former-cyber-executive-of-defrauding-ransomware-victims-by-secretly-paying-hackers/)).

## Timeline

- **2026-11-18**: A federal grand jury in the District of Rhode Island returned the indictment ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
- **2026-11-20**: The defendant was granted pretrial release under strict conditions set by the court ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 

## Details (as Reported)

- The department stated that the company's promotional materials claimed it possessed sophisticated decryption technology that could recover encrypted data without paying the ransom ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
- According to the department, the company told the undercover operative of the Federal Bureau of Investigation that it never negotiated with or paid ransomware attackers ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
- The department stated that the defendant used the proceeds to fund purchases of several properties in Miami, Florida ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
- Assistant Attorney General Tysen Duva stated that the indictment alleges the defendant charged the undercover victim company "more than double what he paid the undercover ransomware actor" ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/colombian-national-indicted-scheme-defraud-victims-ransomware-attacks)). 
- First Assistant United States Attorney Charles Calenda stated that, as alleged, the defendant "preyed on ransomware victims by falsely marketing a solution he did not possess" ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)). 
- TechCrunch reported that a third ransomware negotiator was convicted of helping hackers deploy ransomware against several companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/doj-accuses-former-cyber-executive-of-defrauding-ransomware-victims-by-secretly-paying-hackers/)).  The department stated that he and two other former cybersecurity professionals deployed BlackCat ransomware against victims throughout the United States between April 2023 and November 2023, extorting one victim for approximately $1.2 million in Bitcoin ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/florida-ransomware-negotiator-who-extorted-and-attacked-multiple-us-victims-sentenced-prison)).
- In that separate case, the department announced that the former ransomware negotiator from Florida was sentenced to 70 months for conspiring with the operators of the BlackCat ransomware variant, beginning in April 2023, to extort five different ransomware victims ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/florida-ransomware-negotiator-who-extorted-and-attacked-multiple-us-victims-sentenced-prison)). United States Attorney Jason Reding Quiñones stated that "more than $10 million in criminal proceeds has been seized," and the department set a hearing on restitution for September 17 ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/florida-ransomware-negotiator-who-extorted-and-attacked-multiple-us-victims-sentenced-prison)).
- The department stated that an indictment is merely an allegation and that all defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)).  The department stated that Trial Attorney Christen Gallagher and Assistant United States Attorneys Lee Vilker and Julianne Klein for the District of Rhode Island are prosecuting the case ([Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims)).
