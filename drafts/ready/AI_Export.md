NVIDIA told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers built with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported on November 26, 2026 ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

The Financial Times based its account on three people familiar with the matter, and it reported that partners including Dell and Supermicro had told buyers the chipmaker would not let their orders ship ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)). The newspaper also stated that it was unclear whether the halt would be temporary ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

On November 16, 2026, the United States Bureau of Industry and Security unveiled an interim final rule designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)). The rule took effect on November 19 and requires a United States licence to ship advanced artificial intelligence chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

Al Jazeera reported that the rule is aimed at stopping Chinese artificial intelligence developers from getting around export controls by remotely accessing data centres in third countries, particularly in Southeast Asia ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)). The rule designates the named countries as having an "unacceptable risk of diversion", and the bureau described the change as a logical extension of the existing requirement for companies to obtain a licence to export or transfer chips to firms based in China ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

TweakTown reported that Chinese firms have rented computing capacity from facilities in places such as Singapore and Malaysia, which the outlet described as a way around direct restrictions on buying NVIDIA chips ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114710/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-ai-chips-through-overseas-data-centers/index.html)).

The Next Web reported that Megaspeed, a cloud company based in Singapore, is under criminal investigation in the United States over the possible diversion of chips to China, and that the company denies any wrongdoing ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)). The Department of Commerce and the Department of Justice publicly confirmed that investigators had opened a criminal investigation into Megaspeed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

Singapore, which hosts dozens of data centres, stated on November 17, 2026 that it was "studying the details of this new measure carefully" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

## Details (as Reported)

- Tom's Hardware reported that the rule was developed in collaboration with the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)).
- The rule also restricts data centres that provide remote access to companies headquartered in higher-risk countries such as China, Russia and Iran ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)).
- TweakTown reported that overseas data centres must conduct robust due diligence on customers receiving significant computing capacity, including checking their ownership and control structures ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114710/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-ai-chips-through-overseas-data-centers/index.html)).
- Al Jazeera reported that the ministries of Singapore are engaging affected companies and the relevant authorities of the United States ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).
