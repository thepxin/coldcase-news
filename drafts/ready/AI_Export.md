# NVIDIA reportedly halts Southeast Asian server shipments as United States curbs remote chip access
type: report
then: Existing United States rules already required licences for chip exports to Chinese companies, according to the Bureau of Industry and Security as reported on November 17, 2026 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).
now: NVIDIA told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported on November 26, 2026 ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).
summary: NVIDIA reportedly halted server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia after a United States rule on remote chip access took effect.

NVIDIA told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers built with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported on November 26, 2026 ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

The Financial Times based its account on three people familiar with the matter, and it wrote that partners including Dell and Supermicro told buyers the chipmaker would not let their orders ship ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)). The newspaper also stated that it was unclear whether the halt would be temporary ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

On November 16, 2026, the United States Bureau of Industry and Security unveiled an interim final rule designed to stop existing limits on exports of artificial intelligence hardware from being bypassed through remote computing ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)). The rule took effect on November 19 and requires a United States licence to ship advanced artificial intelligence chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)).

Al Jazeera reported that the rule is aimed at preventing Chinese artificial intelligence developers from getting around export controls by remotely using data centres in third countries, particularly in Southeast Asia ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)). The rule designates the named countries as presenting an "unacceptable risk of diversion", and the bureau described the change as a logical extension of existing rules that already require licences for chip exports to Chinese companies ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

Before the rule, Chinese firms and companies in other restricted countries such as Russia and Iran were able to rent computing capacity from overseas data centres, including those in Singapore and Malaysia, to use high-end NVIDIA chips ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114710/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-ai-chips-through-overseas-data-centers/index.html)).

The Next Web reported that Megaspeed, a cloud company based in Singapore, is under criminal investigation in the United States over the possible diversion of chips to China, and that the company denies wrongdoing ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)). The Department of Commerce and the Department of Justice publicly confirmed the criminal investigation into Megaspeed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

Singapore, which hosts dozens of data centres, stated on November 17, 2026 that it was "studying the details of this new measure carefully" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).

## Details (as Reported)

- Tom's Hardware reported that the rule was developed with the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)).
- The rule also restricts data centres that provide remote access to companies headquartered in higher-risk countries such as China, Russia and Iran ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/new-us-rules-aim-to-restrict-remote-access-to-ai-servers-and-infrastructure-seeks-to-combat-cloud-rental-workarounds-to-gpu-export-controls)).
- TweakTown reported that overseas data centres must vet customers, including their ownership and control structures, when those customers receive significant computing capacity ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114710/us-moves-to-cut-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-ai-chips-through-overseas-data-centers/index.html)).
- Al Jazeera reported that Singapore is engaging with affected companies and with the authorities of the United States ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/us-moves-to-block-chinas-remote-access-to-ai-chips-amid-nvidia-scandal)).
