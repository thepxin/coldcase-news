# \# Satya Nadella explains Microsoft's 60,000 buyout offers, citing a team of 11 developers

# type: report

# 

# Microsoft chief executive officer Satya Nadella explained on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, why the company offered buyouts to 60,000 of its employees in October (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)).

# 

# The company confirmed on Monday, October 26, 2026, that it was offering buyouts to around 60,000 employees, and that eligible staff would receive an email with a link to their Voluntary Exit Offer details that day (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)). An internal note described the Voluntary Exit Offer as new for the company and as "a new choice" for eligible employees (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)). In the same note, the company wrote that it would keep helping people "focus on the work that matters most" and "build new skills" (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)). The buyout terms will be similar to the company's standard severance, and its headcount as of the end of June was 223,000 (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)).

# 

# TechCrunch reported that the offer marked the company's latest effort to cut costs and rework its workforce for the artificial intelligence age, giving it a way to reduce staff without resorting to mass layoffs (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/)).

# 

# Speaking at the DealBook Summit, the chief executive told Andrew Ross Sorkin that the buyouts were meant "to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices" (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He pointed to the team that builds Cowork, the artificial intelligence agent in Microsoft 365 apps, which consists of just 11 developers (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He described the team's work in these words: "They build a software factory that builds the product. They don't build the product" (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)). He added: "That level of direction is what, by the way, is going to happen in all jobs" (\[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)).
